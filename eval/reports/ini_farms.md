# INI Farms

| | |
|---|---|

---

## Facts

- INI Farms was founded in 2009 by Pankaj Khandelwal and Purnima Khandelwal, and works with over 2,000 farmers across Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, and Karnataka for pomegranates and bananas, covering nearly 5,000 acres, plus its own farms totaling 550 acres across seven locations in Maharashtra and Madhya Pradesh. ([source](https://www.iimcal.ac.in/ini-farms-pankaj-khandelwal-pgdcm-5th-batch-fruitful-venture-horticulture-business))
- INI Farms handles over 50,000 tons of fruits annually and operates pan-India in contract farming, aggregation, and supply chain management; its Kimaye brand products are available in major retail chains across 35 countries. ([source](https://inc42.com/buzz/b2b-agritech-startup-agrostar-acquires-ini-farms-to-enter-global-food-supply-chain/))
- On March 31, 2022, AgroStar (India's largest digital farmer network, which had raised $70M in Series D funding in December 2021) announced a 100% cash-and-stock acquisition of INI Farms, explicitly to target the entire fruits and vegetables basket -- starting with onions and vegetables -- and to focus expansion on EU and North America markets. ([source](https://inc42.com/buzz/b2b-agritech-startup-agrostar-acquires-ini-farms-to-enter-global-food-supply-chain/))

## Gaps (2 found — 1 high, 1 medium)

### 🔴 [HIGH] Post-acquisition mandate to expand into new crops and new geographies at once

AgroStar didn't acquire INI Farms to keep doing what it already did -- it explicitly said it's expanding into onions and vegetables, entirely new crop categories, while pushing into EU and North America markets INI Farms hadn't served before. More crops, more farmers, more geographies, all needing the same temperature-controlled handling pomegranates and bananas already require. Scaling a cold chain into new categories is exactly where consistency breaks down without one system tracking every crop and every farm the same way.

## Pitch

**Angle:** AgroStar didn't just buy INI Farms to keep doing what it already does -- it explicitly said it's expanding into onions and vegetables, entirely new crop categories, while pushing into EU and North America markets INI Farms didn't serve before. That's more crops, more farmers, more geographies, all needing the same temperature-controlled handling that pomegranates and bananas already require -- scaling a cold chain into new categories is exactly where consistency breaks down without one system tracking every crop and every farm the same way. GND's ThinxFresh gives INI Farms that consistency from day one of the expansion, not after the first shipment goes wrong.
