## Facts

- Safe & Secure Logistics has operated since 1951, headquartered at Wadala Truck Terminal, Mumbai. ([source](https://safeandsecure.in/pharma-logistics-india/))
- The company operates a 2,700+ owned fleet including 475 reefers (temperature-controlled vehicles), 700+ owned 40ft containers, and 70+ warehouses totaling 185,000 MT of storage capacity. ([source](https://safeandsecure.in/pharma-logistics-india/))
- Safe & Secure offers temperature-controlled transport across three ranges (2C-8C, 15C-25C, -18C) under WHO Good Distribution Practice (GDP) guidelines, with validated temperature mapping and digital logging at 15-minute intervals, reporting a temperature excursion rate of less than 0.3%. ([source](https://safeandsecure.in/pharma-logistics-india/))
- The company covers 27 states and 13,850+ pincodes across India, including major pharma manufacturing hubs Hyderabad, Ahmedabad/Gujarat, and Baddi-BBN (Himachal Pradesh), reporting Rs 1,354 Cr+ group consolidated revenue (FY26), 30,000+ daily shipments, 2,500+ enterprise clients, and a 12,500+ workforce. ([source](https://safeandsecure.in/pharma-logistics-india/))

## Gaps (1 found — 1 medium)

### 🟠 [MEDIUM] 15-minute interval logging, not real-time deviation alerting

Safe & Secure already runs genuinely strong pharma-grade infrastructure -- validated temperature mapping with a 0.3% excursion rate across 30,000+ daily shipments. But digital logging happens every 15 minutes, not instantly: a deviation is caught at the next check-in, not the moment it starts. Even at a 0.3% excursion rate, that's roughly 90 shipments a day where real-time alerting could mean catching and correcting a problem minutes sooner, before it ever registers as a logged excursion.

## Pitch

**Angle:** Safe & Secure already runs pharma-grade cold chain at a scale most competitors can't touch -- 475 reefers, 70+ warehouses, WHO-GDP compliance, and a genuinely strong 0.3% excursion rate across 30,000+ daily shipments. That excursion rate is checked every 15 minutes, though, not instantly -- at that volume, moving from interval logging to real-time deviation alerts is the difference between catching a problem the moment it starts and catching it up to 15 minutes late, across roughly 90 shipments a day that currently register as excursions.
