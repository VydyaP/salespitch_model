## Facts

- Cool Chain Logistics has operated since 2011, describing itself as a young but well-established company in the pharma cold-chain sector, headquartered in Getafe (Madrid) with additional presence in Barcelona. ([source](https://coolchain.es/quienes-somos/))
- The company operates logistics centers in Spain (Madrid-Barcelona), the UK (York), the USA (Indianapolis), and Germany (Darmstadt), with solutions reaching Europe, North America, and South-east Asia. ([source](https://coolchain.es/quienes-somos/))
- Cool Chain Logistics specializes in transporting biological samples, vaccines, and medicines across temperature ranges from ultra-low frozen (-80C/-20C) to refrigerated (2C/8C) to ambient (15C/25C), holding GDP, ISO, and ISTA temperature-qualification certifications. ([source](https://pharma-industry-review.com/companies/cool-chain-logistics-pharmaceutical-cold-chain-services))
- The company uses a reusable high-performance equipment system claiming '100% monitoring of interior temperature' for all shipments, with autonomous temperature maintenance for a minimum of 120 hours -- part of a reuse model that reports 375 tonnes of CO2 reduction and 10,166 fewer boxes destroyed compared to single-use packaging. ([source](https://coolchain.es/quienes-somos/))

## Gaps (2 found — 2 medium)

### 🟠 [MEDIUM] Passive packaging performance, not necessarily active real-time alerting

Cool Chain's '100% monitoring' claim describes a reusable package that autonomously holds temperature for a minimum of 120 hours -- strong passive engineering. But holding temperature on its own is different from someone getting an instant alert the moment a shipment actually drifts. There's no stated system for active, real-time deviation notification during transit, only the packaging's own endurance.

**GND service:** Cold Chain Management (ThinxFresh)

> Based on: un sistema de reutilizacion de equipos de alto rendimiento [with] monitorizacion del 100% de la temperatura interior [and the ability to] mantener la temperatura de manera autonoma un minimo de 120 horas.

### 🟠 [MEDIUM] Four countries, three regulatory regimes, one claimed monitoring standard

Operations span Spain, the UK, the USA, and Germany -- EU GDP, post-Brexit UK, and US FDA requirements all apply depending on the shipment. Maintaining one consistent monitoring and compliance-reporting standard across four physical hubs and three distinct regulatory regimes is a real operational challenge worth confirming, not assuming.

**GND service:** Cold Chain Management (ThinxFresh) + Warehouse Management

> Based on: The organization maintains logistic centers across Spain (Madrid-Barcelona), UK (York), USA (Indianapolis), and Germany (Darmstadt).

## Pitch

**Angle:** Cool Chain Logistics already engineers serious passive cold-chain packaging -- reusable systems that autonomously hold temperature for a minimum of 120 hours, with '100% monitoring of interior temperature' as a stated claim. But that's the packaging performing on its own; it's not the same as an instant alert reaching someone the moment a shipment drifts, across four countries and three regulatory regimes. GND's ThinxFresh adds the active layer on top of Cool Chain's already-strong passive engineering -- turning 'the box can survive 120 hours cold' into 'someone knows within seconds if it doesn't.'

**Opener:** Your reusable packaging holds temperature autonomously for 120 hours -- if a shipment does drift outside that window across Spain, the UK, the US, or Germany, who finds out first, and how fast?
